# OpenRide Manifesto

OpenRide is an open-source mobility marketplace for people who believe technology should connect a market without quietly owning everyone in it.

## 1. Drivers are suppliers, not inventory

Drivers are independent participants in the marketplace. They must be able to decide which work is worth doing and what commercial terms they are willing to accept within transparent legal and safety guardrails.

Declining or skipping an unsuitable request is not, by itself, bad behavior.

## 2. Riders are buyers, not captive demand

Riders should see meaningful alternatives and decide what matters to them: price, pickup speed, trust, vehicle, driver quality or convenience.

A recommendation is useful. Removing choice is not.

## 3. The algorithm is a broker, not a hidden ruler

Matching and ranking should help both sides discover good opportunities.

OpenRide should avoid opaque mechanisms that:
- secretly rewrite driver pricing;
- hide why one driver is shown over another;
- force a race to the lowest possible fare;
- punish normal commercial decisions without explanation.

## 4. Cheapest is not always best

A fair marketplace must not sort only by price.

A driver charging slightly more may be much closer, safer, more reliable or better suited to the rider's needs.

Ranking should balance multiple factors and remain explainable.

## 5. Accepted terms are sacred

Once rider and driver accept a quote, the resulting agreement becomes the commercial source of truth for that ride.

The platform may not silently change the accepted fare because demand, weather, traffic or supply changed afterward.

## 6. Automation must respect user-defined bounds

Drivers may choose manual, automatic or hybrid quoting.

If automatic pricing is used, it operates inside rules and limits the driver has explicitly chosen. Market recommendations are recommendations, not confiscation of pricing control.

## 7. Safety and trust are legitimate platform responsibilities

Open pricing does not mean no rules.

Operators may enforce transparent requirements involving:
- KYC;
- licensing;
- safety;
- fraud;
- prohibited behavior;
- legal price constraints;
- payment integrity;
- service-area rules;
- dispute resolution.

These rules should be visible, auditable and separable from hidden price control.

## 8. Fairness must not become forced allocation

The marketplace may improve exposure for qualified drivers who are systematically under-shown when other factors are similar.

It should not force riders to select a specific driver merely to equalize trip counts.

Fairness means opportunity and transparency, not removing choice.

## 9. The software must remain community-friendly

The long-term goal is not one mandatory OpenRide company.

A driver community, cooperative, local operator or startup should be able to run the software, adapt local policies and contribute improvements upstream.

Architecture decisions should therefore favor:
- self-hosting;
- provider abstractions;
- documented APIs;
- modular business domains;
- transparent configuration;
- migration paths instead of vendor lock-in.

## 10. OpenRide should be infrastructure, not dependency

The project's ambition is to become reusable mobility infrastructure that communities can own together.

> Drivers set their terms. Riders choose. Algorithms connect.
